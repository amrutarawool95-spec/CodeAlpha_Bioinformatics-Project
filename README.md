# 🧬 Bioinformatics Project | CodeAlpha

### Exploring Sequence Analysis, Protein Structures & Biological Databases

A bioinformatics project demonstrating the application of computational tools
and biological databases to protein sequence analysis, evolutionary
conservation, protein structure prediction, and biological data exploration.

**Domain:** Bioinformatics & Computational Biology  
**Project Type:** Academic Mini-Project  
**Focus:** Protein Informatics | Sequence Analysis | Structural Bioinformatics

---

## 📌 Project Overview

Bioinformatics integrates biology, computer science, and computational
methods to analyze biological data and understand molecular systems.

This project explores four fundamental areas of bioinformatics through
sequence analysis, protein structure investigation, and database research.

The project includes:

- 🔬 **Task 1:** DNA/Protein Sequence Analysis using BLAST
- 🧬 **Task 2:** Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA)
- 🧊 **Task 3:** Protein Structure Prediction
- 🗃️ **Task 4:** Comparative Study of Bioinformatics Databases

Human insulin precursor (UniProt accession **P01308**) is used as a
common protein example across the sequence-analysis, alignment, and
structure-analysis tasks.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Understand protein sequence similarity and homology searching.
- Explore evolutionary conservation among related protein sequences.
- Investigate predicted three-dimensional protein structures.
- Understand the roles of major biological databases.
- Develop practical familiarity with bioinformatics resources.
- Interpret computational results in a biological context.
- Understand the importance of data accuracy and evidence-based analysis.

## 🧪 Project Tasks

### Task 1 — DNA/Protein Sequence Analysis Using BLAST

**Objective:** Identify similar protein sequences and explore evolutionary
relationships using sequence similarity searching.

**Tool:** [NCBI BLAST](https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi)

**Protein:** Human insulin precursor  
**UniProt Accession:** P01308  
**Analysis:** Protein BLAST (BLASTp)

**Key Concepts:**
- Sequence similarity searching
- Percentage identity and similarity
- Query coverage
- E-value and alignment score
- Identification of related protein sequences
- Interpretation of sequence conservation

**Report:** [`TASK 1_BLAST_Project_Report.pdf`](TASK%201_BLAST_Project_Report.pdf)

---

### Task 2 — Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA)

**Objective:** Compare insulin precursor protein sequences from five
mammalian species to investigate conserved and variable regions.

**Tool:** [Clustal Omega](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/jdispatcher/msa/clustalo)

**Database:** [UniProt](https://www.uniprot.org/)

**Key Concepts:**
- Multiple sequence alignment
- Conserved amino acid positions
- Sequence variability
- Evolutionary conservation
- Cysteine residues and their structural significance
- Interpretation of alignment conservation

**Report:** [`TASK 2_insulin_precursor_alignment_interpretation.docx`](TASK%202_insulin_precursor_alignment_interpretation.docx)

---

### Task 3 — Protein Structure Prediction

**Objective:** Investigate the predicted three-dimensional structure of
human insulin precursor and understand its structural features.

**Protein:** Human insulin precursor  
**UniProt Accession:** P01308  
**Structure Resource:** [AlphaFold Protein Structure Database](https://alphafold.ebi.ac.uk/)

**Key Concepts:**
- Protein structure prediction
- Three-dimensional molecular structure
- Protein precursor organization
- A-chain and B-chain regions
- C-peptide and signal peptide
- Cysteine residues and disulfide bonds
- Structure-model confidence and limitations

**Report:** [`Task 3_Insulin_Structure_Report.pdf`](Task%203_Insulin_Structure_Report.pdf)

*Note: Predicted structures should be interpreted using their confidence
information and should not automatically be treated as experimentally
determined structures.*

---

### Task 4 — Mini-Project: Comparative Study of Bioinformatics Databases

**Title:** Beyond Biological Data — A Comparative Study of NCBI, UniProt,
and the Protein Data Bank (PDB)

**Objective:** Examine the roles of major biological databases in sequence
retrieval, protein annotation, structural biology, and bioinformatics research.

**Databases Studied:**

| Database | Primary Focus |
|---|---|
| NCBI | Nucleotide sequences, genes, proteins, and biomedical resources |
| UniProt | Protein sequences, functional information, and annotation |
| Protein Data Bank (PDB) | Three-dimensional macromolecular structures |

**Key Concepts:**
- Biological data retrieval
- Protein sequence and functional annotation
- Structural biology
- Cross-database integration
- Database strengths and limitations
- Applications in genomics and drug discovery

**Report:** [`TASK 4_Mini_Project_biological_data_report.pdf`](TASK%204_Mini_Project_biological_data_report.pdf)

## 🔬 Biological Significance

Protein sequence analysis, multiple sequence alignment, and structural
investigation provide complementary perspectives on biological molecules.

Sequence comparisons help identify similarities and differences between
proteins. Multiple sequence alignment helps reveal conserved regions,
while structure resources support the investigation of molecular
organization.

Biological databases provide the underlying sequence, annotation, and
structural information required for these analyses.

Together, these approaches demonstrate how computational tools can
support biological interpretation and further research in bioinformatics.
